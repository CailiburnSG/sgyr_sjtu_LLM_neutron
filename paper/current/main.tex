\begin{abstract}
Neutron-instrumentation records from nuclear power plants are high-volume raw time series for which exhaustive, context-sensitive expert fault labeling is impractical at archive scale, whereas their interpretation must remain traceable to authoritative technical evidence. We present an evidence-constrained diagnostic-assistance workflow rather than an autonomous fault classifier. A zero-prior, tool-using agent first converts unlabeled neutron-current CSV records into an auditable descriptive condition report by applying deterministic probes for data health, morphology, anomalies, lag, extremes, and spatial snapshots. A compact structured alert summary then guides human-specified, multi-stage retrieval over nuclear instrumentation and control manuals, enabling a citation-bearing candidate-interpretation memo. We evaluate the retrieval layer under controlled expansion of a fixed IAEA core library with same-domain supplementary documents, using Chinese and English queries, chunking configurations, and both strict chunk and document-level relevance criteria. In the evaluated setup, cosine similarity can increase while the priority of pre-designated IAEA sources decreases; the primary query-formulation-by-chunking comparison (P0) further shows that query family, language, encoder, and chunking interact rather than yielding a universal query ranking. The results show that similarity alone is insufficient to govern evidence retrieval in this setting and motivate explicit source-priority policies, technically specific query formulation, and human review of diagnostic conclusions.
\end{abstract}

\section{System Design and Methodology}
\label{sec:system}
This section specifies the evidence-constrained transformation contract used throughout the study: a raw measurement record is converted into an auditable descriptive observation packet, compressed into a structured alert summary, linked to retrieved manual evidence, and expressed as a citation-bearing memo for engineering review. The intermediate description makes large, unlabeled archives reviewable without treating the automated chain as a fault classifier; engineering review remains outside that chain and retains decision authority.

The complete transformation contract is summarized in \cref{fig:overview}: it distinguishes the auditable measurement-side artifacts from retrieved documentary evidence and from the external engineering-review decision point.
