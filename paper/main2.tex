\begin{abstract}
Neutron-instrumentation records from nuclear power plants are high-volume raw time series that commonly lack a uniform sample-level fault ground truth, whereas their interpretation must remain traceable to authoritative technical evidence. We present an evidence-constrained diagnostic-assistance workflow rather than an autonomous fault classifier. A zero-prior, tool-using agent first converts unlabeled neutron-current CSV records into an auditable condition report by applying deterministic probes for data health, morphology, anomalies, lag, extremes, and spatial snapshots. A compact structured alert summary then drives multi-stage retrieval over nuclear instrumentation and control manuals, enabling a citation-bearing diagnostic memo. We evaluate the retrieval layer under controlled expansion of a fixed IAEA core library with same-domain supplementary documents, using Chinese and English queries, chunking configurations, and both strict chunk and document-level relevance criteria. In the evaluated setup, cosine similarity can increase while the priority of pre-designated IAEA sources decreases; short Chinese queries are especially sensitive to corpus expansion, whereas the tested chunk-size and overlap changes have a smaller effect. The results show that similarity alone is insufficient to govern evidence retrieval in this setting and motivate terminology-rich queries, explicit source-priority policies, and human review of diagnostic conclusions.
\end{abstract}

\section{System Design and Methodology}
\label{sec:system}
This section specifies the evidence-constrained transformation contract used throughout the study: a raw measurement record is converted into an auditable observation packet, compressed into a structured alert summary, linked to retrieved manual evidence, and expressed as a citation-bearing memo for engineering review. The automated chain produces derived observations and candidate interpretations; engineering review remains outside that chain and retains decision authority.
