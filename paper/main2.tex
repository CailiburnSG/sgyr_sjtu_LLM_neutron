\begin{abstract}
Neutron-instrumentation records from nuclear power plants are high-volume and only weakly labeled, whereas their interpretation must remain traceable to authoritative technical evidence. We present an evidence-constrained diagnostic-assistance workflow rather than an autonomous fault classifier. A zero-prior, tool-using agent first converts neutron-current CSV records into an auditable condition report by applying deterministic probes for data health, morphology, anomalies, lag, extremes, and spatial snapshots. A compact structured alert summary then drives multi-stage retrieval over nuclear instrumentation and control manuals, enabling a citation-bearing diagnostic memo. We evaluate the retrieval layer under controlled expansion of a fixed IAEA core library with same-domain supplementary documents, using Chinese and English queries, chunking configurations, and both strict chunk and document-level relevance criteria. In the evaluated setup, cosine similarity can increase while the priority of pre-designated IAEA sources decreases; short Chinese queries are especially sensitive to corpus expansion, whereas the tested chunk-size and overlap changes have a smaller effect. The results show that similarity alone is insufficient to govern evidence retrieval in this setting and motivate terminology-rich queries, explicit source-priority policies, and human review of diagnostic conclusions.
\end{abstract}
