\subsection{Traceable semantic handoff: from observation to evidence}
At archive scale, the useful intermediate result is not an exhaustive fault label for every signal segment but a bounded, auditable description that tells a reviewer what to investigate next. Across representative workflow executions, the measurement-side probes produce condition reports containing spike episodes, isolated zero events, morphology differences, and strongly co-moving channel groups. These are descriptive signal observations that can be inspected, preserved in an alert summary, and mapped to the technical evidence targets in \cref{tab:alert-query-intents}; they are not independently confirmed equipment faults or root causes.

\Cref{fig:a2-events} and \cref{tab:a2-events} illustrate the form of this semantic handoff for one representative condition-report output. Its seven channels contain 30--34 detected spike episodes (mean 32.4) and each contains 12 isolated zero events. The repeated zero timestamps across channels are a concrete reason to retain cross-channel timing in the description and retrieve signal-integrity or instrumentation context before advancing a physical interpretation. The figure is therefore evidence of an auditable observation inventory, not an archive-wide event-prevalence estimate.

\subsection{Similarity and source priority can diverge}
The corpus-scope results show a consistent deployment risk: increasing the supplementary pool can raise cosine scores while reducing IAEA priority@10. \Cref{fig:scope-priority,tab:scope-aggregate} aggregate the phrase-query results over all 17 available index configurations. For Chinese, mean IAEA priority@10 falls from $1.00$ in the IAEA-only pool to $0.10\pm0.09$ after five supplementary documents and to $0.00$ after ten, while the mean top-1 cosine rises from $0.55$ to $0.63$. English is more gradual: priority@10 remains $0.86\pm0.05$ after five documents and is $0.38\pm0.05$ at 45 documents, while the corresponding cosine stays near $0.69$--$0.70$.

These results do not imply that supplementary documents are irrelevant. Rather, when an application requires IAEA sources to be surfaced first, nearest-neighbor similarity alone is not enough to enforce that policy. Source metadata, filtering, or reranking should be considered before a generated memo is presented as authority-backed evidence.

\subsection{Query formulation interacts with language and chunking}
The P0 results make the interaction visible rather than reducing it to a single encoder or language ranking. \Cref{fig:p0-query-effect} first isolates the effect of replacing the baseline family with manually authored technical-detail queries at $m=10$. Each point is the difference between the family means under one encoder--chunk setting. The English differences range from $-0.03$ to $+0.14$, whereas Chinese differences range from $-0.23$ to $+0.02$. Thus technical detail improves source priority in some settings and degrades it in others; it is not a generally beneficial query expansion.

\subsection{Strict and document-level relevance answer different questions}
Self-retrieval exposes a systematic strict--document-level gap. \Cref{fig:self-retrieval} aggregates first-sentence corpus-decay results over the available result directories and shows the same separation for both Recall@10 and MRR. In one $800/50$ setting, IAEA-only Recall@10 is 0.7625 under exact-node matching and 0.8625 under document-level matching. The gap is expected when overlapping chunks preserve relevant content in a neighboring node. Conversely, terminology-rich domain sentences have substantially stronger document-level Recall@10 in the available results. Evaluation should therefore report both criteria: strict matching measures chunk re-identification, while document-level matching better reflects whether the correct manual was recovered.

\subsection{Fixed-word MiniLM cross-check}
To test whether the observed behavior is tied only to the historical character-based chunking, we also ran the independent fixed-word MiniLM protocol specified in \cref{sec:eval}. It compares a multilingual MiniLM encoder with an English-oriented MiniLM encoder and is reported separately from P0 because the 240-word construction changes the retrieval units.

\Cref{fig:real-embedding-comparison,tab:real-embedding-comparison} show that encoder choice changes cosine scales and source-priority trajectories, but does not collapse the problem into one scalar. The multilingual model gives materially higher Chinese phrase cosine scores (0.62--0.64 versus 0.27 for the English-oriented model), while both models undergo source-priority decay as the supplementary pool grows. Thus multilingual semantic alignment and authoritative-source preference are complementary deployment requirements rather than interchangeable objectives.

\subsection{Discussion and deployment implications}
The pipeline provides traceable observations and evidence retrieval, but it does not replace engineering judgment. In a deployment where particular source families must be surfaced first, the results support terminology-rich condition-report queries together with source metadata, filtering, or reranking. The remaining validation boundaries and threats to interpretation are stated separately in \cref{sec:limitations}.
