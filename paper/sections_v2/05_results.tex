\subsection{Traceable case study: from observation to evidence}
Across representative workflow executions, the measurement-side probes produce condition reports containing synchronized zero events, spike episodes, and strongly co-moving channel groups. One representative record exhibits pronounced differences in channel morphology, several highly collinear detectors, repeated spike episodes on one channel, and zero dropouts on others. \Cref{fig:case-waveforms} displays downsampled traces from this record; all probe outputs are computed at full resolution.

This result demonstrates that the workflow can produce an auditable observation record and use it to organize manual retrieval. It should not be interpreted as independent confirmation of a physical fault or a root cause. In the present study, the memo is an evidence-linked aid for subsequent engineering review; no uniformly aligned fault-label set is available for estimating end-to-end diagnostic accuracy.

\subsection{Similarity and source priority can diverge}
The corpus-scope results show a consistent deployment risk: increasing the supplementary pool can raise cosine scores while reducing IAEA priority@10. \Cref{fig:scope-priority,tab:scope-aggregate} aggregate the phrase-query results over all 17 available index configurations. For Chinese, mean IAEA priority@10 falls from $1.00$ in the IAEA-only pool to $0.10\pm0.09$ after five supplementary documents and to $0.00$ after ten, while the mean top-1 cosine rises from $0.55$ to $0.63$. English is more gradual: priority@10 remains $0.86\pm0.05$ after five documents and is $0.38\pm0.05$ at 45 documents, while the corresponding cosine stays near $0.69$--$0.70$.

These results do not imply that supplementary documents are irrelevant. Rather, when an application requires IAEA sources to be surfaced first, nearest-neighbor similarity alone is not enough to enforce that policy. Source metadata, filtering, or reranking should be considered before a generated memo is presented as authority-backed evidence.

\subsection{Language and query formulation dominate the tested chunking range}
Across the tested index grid, the qualitative language effect is larger than the variation induced by modest changes in chunk length and overlap. Short Chinese prompts are particularly vulnerable to source-priority collapse, whereas terminology-rich English queries retain more core material. This is a finding for the fixed embedding model and corpus, not a claim that one language is intrinsically less retrievable. It supports deriving detailed, terminology-rich queries from the condition report instead of issuing only an ultra-short operator phrase.

\subsection{Strict and document-level relevance answer different questions}
Self-retrieval exposes a systematic strict--document-level gap. \Cref{fig:self-retrieval} aggregates first-sentence corpus-decay results over the available result directories and shows the same separation for both Recall@10 and MRR. In one $800/50$ setting, IAEA-only Recall@10 is 0.7625 under exact-node matching and 0.8625 under document-level matching. The gap is expected when overlapping chunks preserve relevant content in a neighboring node. Conversely, terminology-rich domain sentences have substantially stronger document-level Recall@10 in the available results. Evaluation should therefore report both criteria: strict matching measures chunk re-identification, while document-level matching better reflects whether the correct manual was recovered.

\subsection{Independent embedding-model comparison}
To test whether the observed behavior is tied only to the historical embedding index, we ran the independent embedding-model protocol specified in \cref{sec:eval}. It compares a multilingual MiniLM encoder with an English-oriented MiniLM encoder and is reported separately from the historical \texttt{nomic-embed-text} experiments because the chunk construction differs.

\Cref{fig:real-embedding-comparison,tab:real-embedding-comparison} show that embedding choice changes cosine scales and source-priority trajectories, but does not collapse the problem into one scalar. The multilingual model gives materially higher Chinese phrase cosine scores (0.62--0.64 versus 0.27 for the English-oriented model), while both models undergo source-priority decay as the supplementary pool grows. Thus multilingual semantic alignment and authoritative-source preference are complementary deployment requirements rather than interchangeable objectives.

\subsection{Discussion and deployment implications}
The pipeline provides traceable observations and evidence retrieval, but it does not replace engineering judgment. In a deployment where particular source families must be surfaced first, the results support terminology-rich condition-report queries together with source metadata, filtering, or reranking. The remaining validation boundaries and threats to interpretation are stated separately in \cref{sec:limitations}.
